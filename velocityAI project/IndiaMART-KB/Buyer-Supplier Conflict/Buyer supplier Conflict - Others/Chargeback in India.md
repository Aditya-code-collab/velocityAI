# Chargeback in India

1. Introduction to ChargebackA chargeback is a reversal of a transaction, typically initiated by the cardholder through their bank. This mechanism is designed to protect consumers from unauthorized or fraudulent transactions.In India, chargebacks are governed by the rules and regulations set by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and card networks like Visa, MasterCard, and RuPay. 2. When to Raise a ChargebackA chargeback can be raised under the following circumstances:- Unauthorized transactions: If the cardholder did not authorize the transaction.- Fraudulent transactions: If the cardholder is a victim of fraud.- Billing errors: Incorrect amount charged or duplicate transactions.- Non-receipt of goods/services: Goods or services were not delivered as promised.- Dispute over refund: Refund was promised but not received. 3. Chargeback Procedure1. Identify the Issue:   - Verify the transaction details and confirm the discrepancy.2. Contact the Merchant:   - Attempt to resolve the issue directly with the merchant. This step can often resolve misunderstandings and disputes quickly.3. Document the Dispute:   - Gather all relevant documentation such as receipts, transaction records, communication with the merchant, and any other evidence supporting the claim.4. Notify the Issuing Bank:   - Contact your bank's customer service to inform them about the dispute. This can typically be done through online banking, phone, or by visiting the bank branch.5. Submit a Chargeback Request:   - Fill out the bank's chargeback request form. Provide all necessary details and attach the supporting documentation.6. Bank Review:   - The bank will review the submitted information and may contact the merchant for their side of the story.7. Provisional Credit:   - In some cases, the bank may provide a provisional credit to the cardholder's account while the investigation is ongoing.8. Resolution:   - The bank will make a final decision based on the investigation. If the chargeback is approved, the provisional credit becomes permanent. If denied, the provisional credit is reversed. Necessary Documentation- Copies of transaction receipts.- Communication records with the merchant (emails, messages).- Any proof of return or cancellation of goods/services.- Bank statements highlighting the disputed transaction. 4. Common Reasons for Chargeback1. Unauthorized Transactions:   - Transactions made without the cardholder's consent.2. Fraudulent Transactions:   - Transactions made due to card theft or information compromise.3. Billing Errors:   - Incorrect or duplicate charges on the account.4. Non-Receipt of Goods/Services:   - Items not delivered or services not provided as agreed.5. Defective Goods/Services:   - Goods delivered were damaged or not as described, or services were not performed adequately.6. Refund Issues:   - Promised refunds not processed by the merchant.5. Challenges and Considerations- Time Limits: Chargebacks must typically be raised within a certain period, usually 120 days from the date of the transaction.- Complexity: The chargeback process can be complex and time-consuming, requiring detailed documentation and follow-ups.- Merchant Response: Merchants can dispute the chargeback, providing their own evidence, which can prolong the resolution process.- Potential Fees: Some banks may charge a fee for processing chargebacks, although this is less common in India. 6. ConclusionChargebacks are a crucial consumer protection mechanism that provides recourse in cases of unauthorized, fraudulent, or disputed transactions. Understanding the process and ensuring proper documentation can help in effectively resolving disputes.
