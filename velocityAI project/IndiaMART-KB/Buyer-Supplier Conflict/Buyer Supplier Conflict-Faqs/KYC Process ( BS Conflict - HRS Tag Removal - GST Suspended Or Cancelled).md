# KYC Process ( BS Conflict / HRS Tag Removal / GST Suspended Or Cancelled)

As per the new rules starting from 01 May 2025, we must do a proper KYC for every seller or buyer who is involved in a complaint. Cases Where KYC is Required (In Scope):You must collect KYC documents in the following situations: If the supplier has any of these issues:Marked as a High-Risk Supplier (HRS)Their GST (Goods and Services Tax) is suspended or cancelledThey show high buying and selling activity If a seller or buyer (called Respondent) is added in a complaint case:Collect their KYC documents without fail. If the Respondent is not directly added to the case, but their company details are mentioned in the complaint history:Check if their details are valid using any documents available.If the details are valid, then follow up and ask for KYC documents.If the Respondent has not verified important details like:GST status is showing as Not Verified, Tactical Verified.Mobile number, Email address, or GST Address is not verified.Cases Where KYC is Not Required (Out of Scope):You do not need to ask for KYC documents in the following situations:If the supplier does not have any of these:High-Risk tagSuspended or Cancelled GSTHigh Buy-Sell activityIf the Respondent’s details are not added in the complaint case.If the complaint ticket is from Buyer side (Respondent)” after we already received deal-related documents.Step-by-Step Process to Check and Collect Seller KYCThe executive must follow these steps to complete the KYC (Know Your Customer) process for Respondent sellers or buyers. 1. Check Verification Status:A. GST (Goods and Services Tax) Verification:Go to Weberp >> Search Company >> View MoreCheck the GST status here.B. Mobile Number and Email Verification:Go to Weberp >> Search Company >> View More >> TrustCheck if the mobile number and email ID are verified.C. Bank Account Verification:Go to Weberp >> History >> STS AttachmentLook for an Advance Cheque copy provided to IndiaMART (for paid subscriptions).Match the company name and owner name on the cheque:If names match – it is okay.If names do not match – raise this for verification.2. Follow-Up for KYC Documents:After checking all the above details, the executive must take 3 follow-ups from the Respondent to collect KYC documents.3. If KYC Documents Are Received:The executive must verify the documents, especially:Aadhaar and PAN (Permanent Account Number)Use the official government websites to check if Aadhaar and PAN are linked and valid.https://eportal.incometax.gov.in/iec/foservices/#/pre-login/link-aadhaar-status4. What is KYC?KYC is a process used to verify the identity of users to prevent fraud, money laundering, and other illegal activities. It involves collecting identification documents and personal details.2. Why is KYC required?KYC is mandatory to:Comply with legal and regulatory requirements.Protect user accounts from fraud.Ensure secure transactions on the platform.3. What documents are required for KYC?Mandatory documents include:GST CertificateAdhaar of Owner / Director PAN Card of GST owner Address verification poof - Electricity Bill, Rent AgreementCancelled Cheque - Current A/cIEC Mandatory for exporter clients Drug License Mandatory if dealing in pharmaceutical drugs Optional Docs: Udhyam, ISO, MSME, Certificate of incorporation (CIN), GMP etc.4. How can I get my KYC done?You can get your KYC done by sharing your documents on customercare@indiamart.com For paid sellers, the respective account managers can collect the KYC documents & share with KYC team for validation.5. How long does KYC verification take?KYC verification usually takes 24 to 72 hours after submission, depending on the volume of requests and document clarity.6. What happens if my KYC is rejected?If your KYC is rejected:You will receive an email or notification explaining the reason.You may need to re-submit clearer documents or correct personal information.7. Do I need to redo KYC periodically?KYC may need to be updated:If you receive any BS complaint against your company.If there are regulatory changes.During periodic reviews (usually every 1–2 years).8.
